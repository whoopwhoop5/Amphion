Keywords: voice conversion, accent conversion, emotion conversion, real-time, zero-shot
TL;DR: We propose StyleStream, the first real-time zero-shot voice style (timbre, accent, emotion) conversion system that achieves state-of-the-art conversion performance.
Abstract:
Voice style conversion aims to transform an input utterance to match a target speaker’s timbre, accent, and emotion. A central challenge is disentangling linguistic content from style attributes. While prior work has investigated this disentanglement, the conversion quality remains suboptimal. Moreover, no existing work addresses real-time voice style conversion. To address these limitations, we propose StyleStream, the first streamable zero-shot voice style conversion system that achieves state-of-the-art conversion performance. StyleStream mainly consists of two components: a destylizer, which removes style attributes (timbre, accent and emotion) while retaining linguistic content, and a stylizer, which is a diffusion transformer (DiT) that reintroduces style conditioned on the target speech. Content–style disentanglement is enforced in the destylizer through two mechanisms: (i) automatic speech recognition (ASR) loss that provides text-level supervision, and (ii) a finite scalar quantization (FSQ) module with a compact codebook of size 45, which serves as a strong information bottleneck. The continuous representations preceding the FSQ layer are treated as the content features. By combining chunked-causal attention masking with a non-autoregressive architecture, StyleStream enables real-time voice style conversion with an end-to-end latency of 1 second.

Primary Area: applications to computer vision, audio, language, and other modalities

Comment:
We sincerely thank the reviewers for their insightful feedback and for recognizing StyleStream as a "highly original and technically impressive contribution" (Reviewer HLfV) that addresses a "genuinely new" and "practically valuable" problem (Reviewer FPmA). We are encouraged that the reviewers validated our specific architectural choices as a "clever hybridization" (HLfV) and "well thought out" (FPmA), supported by "well-designed experiments" (Reviewer MYRv) that confirm our system successfully balances high-quality disentanglement with real-time efficiency.

To ensure clarity in the following discussions, we restate the definition of Voice Style Conversion (VSC): generating speech that preserves the source content while transferring the target’s voice style, specifically timbre, accent, and emotion. Within this scope, our core contribution is twofold:

First Streamable VSC: We propose the first framework capable of zero-shot VSC in a real-time, streaming setting. Unlike prior works that are restricted to single-attribute conversion (e.g., timbre-only) or offline inference, StyleStream achieves holistic, real-time VSC.

Representation Insight: We provide rigorous empirical studies justifying the design of the Destylizer (Section 5.3 & 5.4), demonstrating that our specific configuration (ASR + compact codebook + pre-quantization continuous features) delivers significantly cleaner content-style disentanglement than prior state-of-the-art approaches.

We strongly encourage reviewers to view our anonymous video demo (https://stylestream10033.github.io/stylestream/), which showcases the system performing real-time voice style conversion on a local laptop with an RTX 4060 GPU.

We elaborate on the novelty of these contributions and address specific comparisons with prior work below:

1. Limitations of Prior Work

While literature exists for individual voice style attributes, such as Timbre (Seed-VC [1]), Accent (ConvSpeak [2], TokAN [3]), or Emotion (EmoVox [4], PDSN [5]), these approaches face distinct limitations, including reliance on paired data (ConvSpeak), not many-to-many (TokAN), or dependency on external style labels and classifier (PDSN, EmoVox). Crucially, while prior work exists for streaming voice conversion (timbre-only), no existing framework tackles streaming VSC (joint timbre, accent, and emotion transfer).

Currently, Vevo (ICLR 2025) [6] is the only prior work capable of offline VSC (timbre, accent and emotion). Furthermore, Vevo faces two critical limitations:

Suboptimal Disentanglement: As shown in our analysis (Section 5.3), Vevo’s content tokens suffer from style leakage and degraded content intelligibility.
Streaming Incompatibility: Vevo relies on an LLM (autoregressive transformer) for duration modeling. The inherent input-output length mismatch in LLM-based generation makes real-time speech-to-speech streaming fundamentally difficult.
2. Destylizer vs. CosyVoice 2

Regarding the Destylizer’s architectural similarity to CosyVoice 2 [7], we emphasize that our contribution addresses a fundamental representation learning challenge. While CosyVoice 2 employs standard large codebooks that lead to significant style leakage (as evidenced in Table 5), we identify a critical representational insight: a compact codebook (size 45) combined with continuous pre-quantization features creates the necessary information bottleneck to strip away style while preserving content. This design is not arbitrary; it is rigorously validated by our ablation studies (Tables 6 & 7). We believe the in-depth analysis of style leakage (Section 5.3) and architectural design (Section 5.4) offers valuable, generalizable insights to the speech community regarding robust content-style disentanglement.

Summary:
The paper introduces StyleStream, a framework for real-time zero-shot voice style conversion capable of transforming an input utterance’s timbre, accent, and emotion to match a target speaker’s style, while preserving the original linguistic content. The proposed system combines a destylizer trained with ASR supervision and a compact finite scalar quantization (FSQ) bottleneck to achieve cleaner content–style disentanglement, and a diffusion transformer stylizer for style reconstruction. The system achieves ~1s end-to-end latency, representing a significant advance toward practical real-time style conversion. Experimental results show that StyleStream outperforms previous methods (CosyVoice 2.0, Vevo, SeedVC) across multiple style similarity and MOS metrics.

Soundness: 3: good
Presentation: 3: good
Contribution: 3: good
Strengths:
Real-time voice style conversion is an important and challenging problem that requires transferring not only timbre but also higher-level stylistic cues (accent and emotion). Tackling this in a streaming setting is timely and practically valuable.
Experimental results are strong, and the demo samples sound convincing, with clear style transfer and good intelligibility.
The method design is well thought out: combining ASR loss with a small quantization codebook effectively improves the destylizer’s disentanglement ability. The use of continuous pre-quantization features is a useful empirical trick that improves performance and is well justified by ablation studies.
Weaknesses:
The main contribution lies in the task and empirical achievement—a functioning real-time voice style conversion system—rather than in methodological novelty. The ASR-supervised tokenizer and DiT-based spectrogram generator follow ideas already seen in recent works (e.g., CosyVoice2, E2-TTS, F5-TTS). A stronger baseline would help: a simple cascaded ASR + zero-shot TTS pipeline could demonstrate more clearly where StyleStream’s advantages lie (e.g., latency, style fidelity).

The experimental scope could be expanded. Multilingual and higher-sampling-rate (e.g., 24 kHz) experiments would make the evaluation more solid and test the model’s generalization.

Thank you for your insightful review. We are encouraged that you think our work is "timely and practically valuable". Here we will address your concerns as follows:

Hence, the paper’s conceptual originality for the machine learning community may be somewhat limited, though it is impactful for speech research.

We appreciate the recognition of our system's empirical success. However, we respectfully argue that our contribution extends beyond system integration and addresses a fundamental representation learning challenge. While we leverage established modules (ASR supervision, FSQ), our core novelty lies in discovering a specific disentangled representation that standard approaches fail to capture.

Specifically, we diverge from standard practices in two critical ways:

1. Information Bottleneck: Unlike CosyVoice 2, which uses large codebooks that inadvertently encode style, we identify that a highly compact codebook (size 45) is essential for stripping away style information.

2. Continuous Feature Space: We propose the use of pre-quantization continuous features rather than discrete tokens. Our ablation studies (Section 5.4) demonstrate that this design choice is not merely a hyperparameter tune, but a structural requirement for achieving clean content-style disentanglement without sacrificing intelligibility.

Therefore, our novelty is the discovery of this specific disentangled content representation. Crucially, this robust disentanglement eliminates the need for complex autoregressive error correction, directly enabling the first streamable zero-shot VSC framework.

A stronger baseline would help: a simple cascaded ASR + zero-shot TTS pipeline could demonstrate more clearly where StyleStream’s advantages lie (e.g., latency, style fidelity).

We appreciate the suggestion to benchmark against a stronger baseline. We have constructed an offline cascaded ASR+TTS pipeline using Whisper-large-v3 and CosyVoice 2.0. The comparative results are presented below:

Model	WER (%) ↓	S-SIM ↑	A-SIM ↑	E-SIM ↑
StyleStream (streaming)	15.3	0.852	0.635	0.803
StyleStream (offline)	9.2	0.856	0.640	0.827
ASR+TTS (offline)	7.4	0.840	0.624	0.740
In the offline setting, the cascaded baseline is comparable to StyleStream. While the ASR+TTS baseline achieves a slightly lower WER (benefiting from explicit text input to TTS, which enforces content stability provided the ASR transcription is accurate), StyleStream demonstrates superior performance in style fidelity. Specifically, StyleStream achieves higher similarity scores across timbre, accent, and emotion.

However, we emphasize that while ASR+TTS is viable offline, it faces fundamental structural limitations in streaming settings:

1. Input-Output Synchronization & Duration Mismatch: The cascaded baseline relies on text as an intermediate representation, which inherently discards duration information. Consequently, the autoregressive TTS generates speech with durations uncorrelated to the input source, leading to significant synchronization drift (overlap or heavy delays) that breaks the streaming pipeline. In contrast, StyleStream is designed with a fully non-autoregressive architecture, ensuring input-output alignment is maintained naturally during streaming.

2. Latency & Error Propagation: A cascaded streaming system is bottlenecked by the ASR module's latency and stability. The TTS module risks starvation while waiting for the ASR to commit to uncertain tokens, causing audio glitches or compounded latency. Furthermore, any ASR transcription errors permanently corrupt the TTS output. StyleStream avoids this by utilizing continuous content features from the streaming Destylizer. This allows the model to adapt to acoustic input continuously without the latency penalties or hard-threshold errors associated with discrete text tokenization.

As scaling to 24 kHz is a standard engineering integration orthogonal to our primary contributions in streaming mechanics and disentanglement, we believe the current evaluation fully validates the proposed method.

Q1: In Table 1, for CosyVoice 2, Vevo, and Vevo 1.5, did the authors use only the flow-matching (FM) component for zero-shot conversion, or the full AR + FM pipeline?

We utilized the official inference implementations for all baselines. However, the inference pipelines differ based on the architecture of each model:

1. Vevo and Vevo 1.5: We utilized the full pipeline (LLM + DiT). For these models, the official voice style conversion pipeline involves extracting content tokens from the source audio, passing them through the LLM to generate content-style tokens, and finally using the DiT for waveform generation. Because the LLM is involved in the intermediate stage to model the target style, these baselines perform full voice style conversion, not just timbre conversion.

2. CosyVoice 2: We utilized the Flow Matching/DiT component only. We clarify that the official CosyVoice 2 architecture can just support timbre-only voice conversion. It does not support token-to-token translation via its LLM, since its LLM is designed strictly for text-to-token. Consequently, the official pipeline bypasses the LLM: it extracts speech tokens from the source and feeds them directly into the DiT, conditioned on the target speaker embedding and context mel-spectrogram. We acknowledge the reviewer’s insight that this setup primarily performs timbre transfer, because the source speech tokens in CosyVoice 2 are not fully disentangled from the source style, as shown in Section 5.3.

Q2: Regarding the streaming design, how exactly is DiT modified to support causal or chunked inference? Is the approach similar to that of CosyVoice 2?

Yes, the approach shares similarities with CosyVoice 2 regarding the masking strategy. As detailed in Section 3.4, we train the DiT using a chunked causal attention mask to enforce causality. For the inference stage (Section 5.2), we maintain a 5-second content ring buffer. This buffer feeds the DiT the necessary historical context for the current chunk, ensuring smooth transitions and stability during streaming generation.

Q3: Could the destylizer benefit from being trained on the same large-scale dataset as the stylizer (e.g., Emilia)?

We thank the reviewer for the suggestion regarding data scaling. To investigate this, we conducted experiments where we trained the Destylizer on the Emilia-EN dataset and retrained the Stylizer on top of it. The comparative results are shown below:

Training data	WER (%) ↓	S-SIM ↑	A-SIM ↑	E-SIM ↑
LibriTTS + MSP-Podcast + GLOBE (1300 hours)	10.7	0.837	0.626	0.733
Emilia (50k hours)	10.3	0.816	0.594	0.687
As shown, scaling the Destylizer to 50k hours yields no significant overall improvement. While there is a marginal gain in content preservation (WER drops from 10.7% to 10.3%), the style disentanglement metrics (S-SIM, A-SIM) actually show a slight degradation. We attribute this saturation to two factors:

1. Task Nature (Discriminative vs. Generative): The Destylizer performs a discriminative extraction task (compressing speech into content features via ASR supervision), which differs fundamentally from the Stylizer's generative task. While generative models (DiT) require massive data to capture long-tail acoustic distributions, the Destylizer's task saturates much faster. Once the model learns robust content-style disentanglement, adding more data yields diminishing returns.

2. Strategic Data Sufficiency: Our 1.3k-hour dataset was constructed to maximize style variance, not just total duration. By combining GLOBE (high accent diversity), MSP-Podcast (high emotion diversity), and LibriTTS (clean timbre), this dataset already covers the necessary manifold of style variations required for the bottleneck to learn disentanglement. Adding the massive "in-the-wild" Emilia data does not significantly expand this style manifold relative to the Destylizer's needs, and as observed, may even introduce noise that slightly hampers style isolation.

Therefore, we conclude that the 1.3k-hour dataset is the optimal efficiency point for the Destylizer.

Summary:
This paper introduces StyleStream, the first system designed for real-time, zero-shot voice style conversion, which aims to transform a source utterance to match a target speaker's timbre, accent, and emotion. The work addresses two primary limitations of prior methods: suboptimal conversion quality due to incomplete disentanglement of linguistic content from style, and the lack of any existing real-time solution for this task. The main contributions of the paper are:

1.A Novel Two-Stage Architecture: StyleStream consists of a destylizer and a stylizer. The destylizer's key function is to achieve clean content-style disentanglement. It does this by combining two mechanisms: (1) text-level supervision via an Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) loss, which forces the model to preserve only linguistically relevant information, and (2) a strong information bottleneck implemented with a compact Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) module (codebook size of 45). Crucially, it uses the continuous features before the quantization step as the final content representation to maintain high fidelity. The stylizer is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that reintroduces the target style onto the clean content features using a spectrogram inpainting objective.  

2.First Real-Time Implementation: By using a non-autoregressive architecture and applying chunked-causal attention masking, StyleStream is the first framework to enable streaming voice style conversion. It achieves an end-to-end latency of approximately 1 second on a single GPU.  

3.State-of-the-Art Performance: The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art results in zero-shot voice style conversion. Experimental evaluations show that StyleStream significantly outperforms previous models in preserving content intelligibility (lowest Word Error Rate) while demonstrating substantially higher similarity to the target speaker's accent and emotion.

Presentation: 2: fair
Contribution: 3: good
Strengths:
The paper presents a highly original and technically impressive contribution to speech generation research. It introduces StyleStream, the first framework capable of real-time zero-shot voice style conversion that jointly transfers timbre, accent, and emotion. This problem setting is genuinely new: previous real-time systems only handled timbre, while prior style-conversion frameworks were all offline. The proposed architecture achieves this by combining two complementary modules — a destylizer that performs ASR-supervised content–style disentanglement with finite scalar quantization (FSQ), and a stylizer based on a diffusion transformer (DiT) that reintroduces the target style in a streamable manner. The integration of these components demonstrates clear technical originality and conceptual coherence. The use of ASR loss together with a compact FSQ bottleneck represents a clever hybridization of ideas from CosyVoice and SoftVC, and provides an elegant, explainable mechanism for disentangling linguistic content from paralinguistic style.

From the standpoint of technical quality, the paper is thorough and well executed. The experimental design is robust: the authors compare against strong recent baselines including Vevo, Vevo 1.5, CosyVoice 2.0, FACodec, and SeedVC, using both objective metrics (WER, speaker/accent/emotion similarity) and subjective MOS/SMOS ratings. The ablation studies are comprehensive and illuminating. For instance, the authors carefully vary the FSQ bottleneck size, the type of content features, and the use of the style embedding, showing quantitatively how each factor influences intelligibility and style fidelity. The methodology for streaming inference is also well justified — chunked-causal masking, causal convolutions, and teacher–student distillation together enable an end-to-end latency around one second without severely compromising perceptual quality. These results, combined with clear and well-documented training configurations, strongly support the claimed contributions.

In terms of clarity, the paper is mostly well organized and easy to follow. Figures 1–3 effectively illustrate the pipeline, and the motivation for each design choice is well connected to empirical evidence. The authors clearly articulate why continuous pre-quantization features offer a better balance between content preservation and disentanglement, and the discussion in Section 5.3 provides convincing support for this claim. Although the model is complex, the presentation remains coherent and the technical details are traceable.

Weaknesses:
Despite its merits, the paper has several areas that could be improved.

The most notable limitation is that all experiments are conducted on English-only datasets. Since accent and emotion transfer are highly language-sensitive, the current evidence does not demonstrate whether StyleStream generalizes across languages or linguistic families.

The latency–quality trade-off is not analyzed in sufficient depth. The paper reports an average end-to-end latency of roughly 1 s on an NVIDIA A6000 but does not explore how latency scales with chunk size or hardware constraints.

The effects of individual streaming modifications (chunked-causal attention, causal convolutions, and the distillation procedure) are not disentangled, making it unclear which component contributes most to the performance gap between the streaming and offline variants.

Operator-Level Modifications: Regarding the individual ablation of specific streaming operators (chunked-causal attention, causal convolutions, distillation), we clarify that these components are not independent algorithmic additions but unified prerequisites for causality. In a streaming framework, causality is binary: the system must be strictly causal at every layer to function. Removing one component (e.g., reverting causal convolutions to non-causal while keeping chunked attention) would violate the streaming constraint and require future information, rendering the system non-streamable. Therefore, these modifications function as an indivisible architectural package necessary to enable valid streaming inference, rather than independent factors that can be meaningfully ablated in isolation.
